## Summary

Trace (#1060) as we agreed by mail: this takes the place of the section with Dark/Light, Threshold and **Trace outline** in the reference image's settings – the outlines come as curves and corners with a number of points you set, previewed before they are applied – and adds the object before its background, picked areas, Cut away, the magnetic lasso and the same Trace in the vase drawing. The MCP's trace of a reference image (`layerling_reference_image` with `trace`) stays as it is.

![Trace in 1.127.0 and with this pull request on the same pictures and threshold: the cat 182 points of straight lines against 26 points of curves and corners, the word 442 against 101](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/marcus040473/layerling/98f496eaf9ade223a1d45914ba6cfdfddcdf4552/trace/compare-1127.png)

- **Trace in the sketch:** **Trace** in the reference image's settings traces the outline of the shape in the picture – a silhouette, a logo, a word – and shows it in red over the picture: square points for corners, round ones for smooth points. Settings: **Dark shape / Light shape**, **Threshold**, **Points** (how many the outlines get together, from the fewest the shape allows to a close fit; the hint under it gives the count and the corners), **Corner from** (degrees; 180 makes none), **Leave out specks up to** (mm) and **Keep holes** (off: only the outermost outlines). **Apply** puts the outlines into the sketch where the picture lies – its size, turn and crop, only the part it shows – selected, one step to undo. They are ordinary sketch points: corners plain or split between curves, smooth points with their handles in line, so they move, curve and remove like drawn ones, and holes are outlines inside others as usual.
- **The object before its background:** under **Shape**, **Object** beside Dark shape and Light shape. The background is the colour at the picture's edge (the middle of its pixels' colours); all that is close to it and joined to the edge is background, everything else is one outline, whatever its colours – a toy photographed on white comes out as one clean outline instead of dozens round its logo, its highlights and its wheels. **Threshold** sets how far a colour must lie from the background's. A colourful picture on an even background starts this way, without holes; with **Keep holes**, the patches of background colour the object encloses are its holes.
- **Pick areas:** a click on the picture outlines the patch of like colour round the spot (**Colour tolerance**), as a hole – the window of a train, say. An area that reaches the picture's edge is the background and is not taken (the card says so).
- **Cut away:** clicks on the picture set the corners of a part to take from the shape before its outline is found, whatever its colour – a shadow under a toy as dark as the wheels above it, which no threshold or colour tells apart. A click on the first corner, a double click or Enter closes it, Escape throws the corners away; it shows dashed over the picture. Inside the shape a cut is a hole whatever the holes are; at its edge the outline runs along the cut; on the object it lies on the background.
- **Magnetic lasso:** where no colour tells a shape from the rest (a tree beside a house, a figure before a busy background), clicks roughly along its edge lay a line that follows the edge between them by itself, round its bends too; while the pointer moves, the line on to it shows dashed. It closes like a cut. Each click moves onto the strongest edge within a few pixels; between two the way is the cheapest through the picture, each pixel the cheaper the stronger the edge it lies on (Sobel on the three channels, against a strong edge of this picture; Dijkstra in a box round the two clicks, pulled a little to their straight line, so it runs straight where there is no edge), after intelligent scissors (Mortensen and Barrett 1995). Every region drawn by hand, lasso or cut, is listed with **Take in** (it joins the shape, whatever its colour) or **Cut away**; a lasso starts taken in, a cut cut away. Under **Shape**, **Lasso only** makes the shape of what is taken in alone, nothing of the picture's own (no threshold, no holes; choosing it starts the lasso).
- **Holes and areas:** each hole, clicked area, lasso and cut is listed; pointing at one shows it stronger in the preview, ✕ takes it out (holes taken out can be brought back), and with **Cut out** off it is drawn as a construction line instead of a hole. Holes are named by where they lie, so a choice outlasts a small change of the threshold; an island in a hole goes with its hole; an area clicked in a hole takes its place.
- **No crossing outlines:** fitted loosely, two outlines a pixel or two apart (the two edges of a drawn line) could cross, and crossing outlines make no body. Outlines that cross another or themselves are fitted again more closely, down to the closest fit (a grid of their steps finds the crossings); the card says when that gave more points than asked for.
- **The preview covers what falls away** – around the outlines, in the holes, what is cut away – in the plate's colour, so only what stays shows of the picture.
- **Trace in the vase drawing:** beside **Take outline**, the photo row gets **Trace**: the body form from the photo with **Threshold** (how far a colour lies from the background's), **Points** (3 to 40) and **Corner from**, shown dashed in red over the photo before **Apply** makes it the vase's. The photo follows the object as the threshold changes it; Cancel lays it back. Trace there has the **Magnetic lasso** too: clicks roughly round the vase lay a line along the photo's edges, as in the sketch, and closed it is the object instead of the search – so a vase before a shelf or in a room, where the search finds nothing (and Trace now starts with the lasso), can be traced as well. **Discard lasso** lets the search look again. The vase drawing's module stays plain JavaScript: the window hands it the way along the edges (`edgeLasso`, like `readMesh`).
- **The tracer** (`pictureTrace.ts`) builds on `rasterTrace`'s outline (its field, softening and marching squares, now exported): the outline walked in even steps; a corner where the line turns by more than the corner angle within three pixels while it runs on straight either side (a tight curve turns as much, but spread out), placed where the two sides meet; between the corners cubic Béziers fitted by least squares and split where they stray (after Schneider, Graphics Gems 1990), as loosely as the number of points allows (a bisection on the tolerance). Long straight stretches are lines: within 0.6 px of their line and not bowing to one side (the parabola that fits their steps best rises at most 0.12 px over them, so a large circle is not taken in straights), at least 4 % of the picture's longer side and four times the tolerance long; the curves between them leave and meet them along them, so the slopes of a roof with a rounded top stay straight and the top is a short rounding, a point at either end. Straights that meet nearly in line are joined where the tolerance allows. No new dependency.
- `findObject` takes a threshold and `profileFromSamples` a number of points and a corner angle; without them both work as before. `outlineObject` makes the same object from an outline drawn round it (its extent per row, through the same rim and base handling, now shared as `objectFromRows`).
- Guide: three paragraphs in "A picture as template" (chapter 7) and two sentences in the vase drawing (chapter 3). Texts in English, German, French, Dutch, Russian, Czech, Slovenian and Danish.

## Notes

- **Storage:** nothing new. Trace only adds ordinary points and sides (construction lines for areas not cut out) to the sketch, or a body form to the vase.
- The idea came from the trace tools of laser programs (LightBurn and Inkscape trace with Potrace); this is written from scratch – Potrace's code is GPL – on the outline `rasterTrace` already finds.
- The lasso's way is looked for at most once a frame while the pointer moves (a few ms in a box round the two clicks; the picture's edges are found once, under 0.1 s for 1000 pixels).
- The picture is traced in the main thread, at most 1000 pixels along its longer side, again once a slider rests for 90 ms – the outlines and the range of points alike, the range kept while only the number of points changes; a busy picture takes up to a few hundred ms per change. **Apply** takes the trace for the settings as they are now: when a change is still waiting for its pause, it traces at once rather than taking the one shown (the vase drawing's Apply does the same with its slider's retrace). Moving the tracing and the lasso's search into a worker would keep even a busy picture from holding the page up; I left that for a later step.
- Polish misses the new sentences; the translations other than English and German are mine and worth a native reader.
- **How it meets #1060:** `sketchImageTrace.ts` holds both – your `croppedPixels`, `tracedImageOutlines` and `imageTraceProfile` for the MCP, and a shared `shownPixels` that cuts the part an image shows for both (`tracedImageOutlines` uses it now). `sketchTraceOfImage` went with the section; its crop and light-part tests run on `tracedImageOutlines` and `imageTraceProfile` instead. Your `sketch.traceImage`, `sketch.traceThreshold` and `status.sketchImageTraced` (outlines and lines) are used as they are, in every language; `sketch.traceImageHint` carries the new button's hint in the eight languages it has here and keeps your text in the others; `sketch.traceImageButton` is gone. In chapter 7 the new paragraphs keep your tip on a border round the outline; the other languages' paragraph points at **Apply**, and chapter 10 at **Trace**.
- The MCP's trace could take the curves as well, as a follow-up if you like.
