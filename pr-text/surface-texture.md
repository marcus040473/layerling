## Summary

- **Surface texture** lays a relief onto any body, for grip or for looks: a card in the shape's settings, and a button of that name in the **Modify** group that opens it (lit while it is open; the command search finds it too).
- Twelve patterns: **Grooves**, **Knurling**, **Honeycomb**, **Bumps** (half ball, pointed cone, softly rounded, flat on top), **Cells** (irregular, Voronoi), **Knitting**, **Rope**, **Basket weave**, **Grain** (fine, as if blasted), **Image** (a grey height map: twelve drawn in code that tile without a seam, or one's own picture, kept with the design in grey at most 256 points a side), **Cracks (kintsugi)** and **Text (stuck on)**.
- Laid around an axis for round bodies (the spacing is rounded so a whole number goes round and the pattern closes without a seam; the axis is fitted to the walls, so a cup's handle does not pull it aside), from all round for angular ones (each flat face is cut along its own pattern and ends at a straight border), or flat along an axis; **Automatic** picks. Under **More**: width and profile, slant, which walls, how far bottom and top stay smooth, a band of the height, sharp edges kept smooth, fade-in and detail.
- The mesh is cut along the pattern rather than refined evenly (grooves into strips at the places of their profile, knurling along both groove families, honeycomb and cells along their joints, bumps as rings round their middle, letters along their outlines), so a pointed profile comes out exactly and joints are straight instead of jagged. It is computed on a worker of its own; the body becomes a mesh with the relief, and undo takes it back.
- **Cracks** walk on the surface itself, so they go over a box's edge and never through a thin wall, and can fill their notch with a closed gold body as a part of its own for a second filament. **Text** is laid on by unrolling the surface round its place, like a label pressed on: the letters keep their size on a belly, a slanted wall or over an edge.
- **Texture masks**: a box, cylinder, sphere, cone or pyramid marked as a mask keeps the texture away from where it reaches into another body, or is the only place it goes.
- **Changed or removed later**: a textured body keeps the body under it, also in the saved project; the card shows the settings it was made with, **Change texture** makes it again, **Remove texture** gives the plain body back.
- MCP: `layerling_texture_surface` (all settings, `masks`, `remove: true`), and a paragraph in SKILL.md.
- Guide: a section "A surface texture" in chapter 3 and a row in chapter 14. Panel texts and that section in English, German, Dutch, Czech, French, Slovenian and Russian; the other languages show the English text.

The body becomes a mesh with the relief, computed on a worker of its own (a 60 mm cup takes about a second); like wrapping around a cylinder it is a step, and undo takes it back. The MCP bridge has it as `layerling_texture_surface`. Described in chapter 3 of the guide; the panel and that section are in English, German, Dutch, Czech, French, Slovenian and Russian (the languages other than English and German drafted by the contributor and worth a native reader), the other languages show the English text.
- **Cracks as in kintsugi, with gold as a part of its own:** The surface texture's pattern **Cracks (kintsugi)** grows lines across the body's surface with kinks, branches, chipped edges and a width that tapers to their end - from the top rim, the bottom, the rim towards the middle of a plate, or anywhere, as a wandering crack or as lightning. The lines walk on the surface itself, so they go over a box's edge as round a cup, and never through a thin wall onto its other side. Only the band round them is refined. **Gold filling as a part of its own** fills their notch with a closed body in gold for a second filament, a little below the surface; it goes along when the texture is changed or removed. The same seed gives the same cracks. The MCP tool takes the crack settings and returns the gold.
- **Text stuck on a surface:** The pattern **Text (stuck on)** lays words in any of the text fonts onto the body like a label pressed on. The surface round the place is unrolled, so the letters keep their size on a vase's belly, a slanted wall or over the edge of a box, engraved or raised; only the window round the text is refined, and the mesh is cut along the letters' outlines, so their edges stay clean. **Around** (0° the front, 90° the right; with the axis on X or Y, 0° the top) and **Height** place it, **Turned** turns it. Over a real corner the text tears like a sticker, and the status line says so. The MCP tool takes `pattern: "text"` with `textContent`, `textFont`, `textSize`, `textAround`, `textHeight` and `textTurn`.
- **Texture masks: keep part of a body smooth, or texture only a part:** A box, cylinder, sphere, cone or pyramid can be a **Texture mask** in its properties: where it reaches into another body, it **Keeps the texture away**, or with **Texture only here** that body's texture goes only there. It turns transparent and counts when the other body gets its surface texture, also while it is hidden (hide or delete it before printing); the pattern fades in at its border as at every other place that stays smooth, and the status line names the masks taken into account. A mask is described by the planes of its faces, so it works for these convex bodies at any turn and size. The MCP tool takes `masks` as a list of bodies and modes, and otherwise uses the bodies marked as masks.
- **A surface texture can be changed or taken off later:** A textured body keeps the body under its relief, also in the saved project. Selected again, its **Surface texture** card shows the settings it was made with: **Change texture** makes the texture again on that body with new settings, where and as large as the textured body stands now, and **Remove texture** gives the plain body back. The body under the texture is kept as fillets and hollowing keep theirs, as an entry of the body's treatments (kind `surfaceTexture`, with the settings), so a texture also shows up in their list, to be taken back there. The MCP tool does the same on a textured body: called again it changes the texture (settings not passed stay as they were), and `remove: true` takes it off.
